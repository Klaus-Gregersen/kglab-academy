# A short beginner observation session

This is a guide for a future session, not a report of research already conducted. Automated browser tests check behavior, but cannot establish whether a complete beginner understands the experience.

Invite a willing beginner to spend about 15–25 minutes in a fresh browser profile, so existing progress is preserved. Explain that you are checking the lab, not their ability. Ask them to say what they expect before acting. Let them stop whenever they want. Do not collect names, recordings, or other personal information by default.

## Prompts

1. “What do you think this page lets you do? Show me where you would start.” Observe whether the command input is obvious.
2. Before the first command: “In your own words, what will `pwd` tell you?” Observe whether the introduction explains a directory and path.
3. “Use whatever help you would normally use.” Observe whether they discover the objective walkthrough, pause/transcript, lightbulb, and settings without being directed to each control.
4. During navigation: “Where are you now? Where would a new file appear?” Watch whether they understand that `mkdir` creates a folder and `cd` moves into it. Do not supply the answer first.
5. After a couple of missions: “Can you return to an explanation you've already used?” Check whether completed-mission replay is discoverable and returning to practice is clear.
6. At Build Your Launchpad: “Make this project in whatever way seems right to you.” Observe whether they can combine commands and check their work before opening a solution.
7. “Find another free resource for a topic you want to learn. Save it for later.” Then ask them to upvote it. Observe whether they understand that bookmarks are personal, upvotes affect shared ordering, and clicking the arrow again removes their vote. Ask them to find the link under Most upvoted and A–Z. Misunderstanding here means the wording needs work.

Avoid stepping in immediately. If they ask for help, first ask what they expected or what they think the current result means. Offer support when frustration replaces useful exploration; this is not a test of endurance.

## Notes template

For each moment, record only:

- Task and device/viewport category.
- What the person expected, what they tried, and what happened.
- Whether they recovered independently, used optional help, or needed you.
- A concrete proposed improvement and how to check it in another session.

Prioritize confusion about location, file creation, completion, or help over cosmetic preferences. Do not claim a feature is proven usable from one person's reaction. Keep a short record of what was actually observed and what remains an assumption.
