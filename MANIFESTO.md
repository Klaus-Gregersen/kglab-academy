# KG LAB Academy

**Build. Learn. Repeat.**

We want beginners to feel confident using real tools. The lab should give people a clear starting point, room to experiment, and an explanation when something clicks.

## Understanding comes first

Explain what a command does before asking someone to use it. Let learners watch an example, try it themselves, make mistakes, and return to an explanation. Offer help without rushing them. Measure progress by what they understand and can do independently.

## Keep the door open

KG LAB Academy is free to use. No paywalls, unnecessary accounts, artificial restrictions, or pressure to keep learning here. Prefer a small, clear experience over another feature that gets in the way.

## Recommend what helps

Good learning happens in many places. If another project explains a subject well, point people toward it. Credit its creators and tell learners why it might help, who it is for, and what they need to begin.

The community shelf welcomes recommendations for free repositories, courses, guides, books, and labs in different categories. A useful explanation matters more than a popularity score. Be clear about registration, optional paid extras, software requirements, and the date access was checked. Free learning material should be available without purchasing a subscription; disclose any separate setup costs or licensing terms.

## Pass experience on

Someone's small suggestion can change how another person works. Share those shortcuts, clearer explanations, and useful links. Improving the lessons and documentation is part of learning here, through branches, commits, and pull requests.

Maintain the collection openly. Review recommendations, correct broken links, and welcome reasons to change our minds. Let learners influence the shelf through shared upvotes that bring useful resources to the top. Keep personal bookmarks separate. Votes guide discovery and curation; new links still receive an open review. Never invent votes or present editorial picks as community consensus.

## Build with care

Keep the terminal simulated and the interface accessible. Preserve a learner's work, let them choose when to reveal help, and respect reduced motion. Ask beginners to try the lab and use their observed difficulties to make the next version clearer.

The goal is confidence that travels beyond the lab.
